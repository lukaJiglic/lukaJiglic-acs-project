## (b) Candidate historical decks

### The pattern itself

- The pattern dates from 1815 (earliest known Vienna deck). Source: Wikipedia, Commons category note.
- IPCS splits it into three types. Type A and Type B were established by 1865. Type C appeared around 1890.
- Type C is the one used in Slovenia, Austria and Czechia. Source: Wikipedia.
- IPCS lists Ludwig Jaeger (Vienna, c. 1890) and Ferd. Piatnik & Söhne (c. 1890 onward) as Type C makers.
- WOPC and the V&A credit the Piatnik design to the engraver J. (Josef) Neumayer, "worked c. 1890". No birth or death date was found.
- A pattern as such is not a protected work. Each drawn version is. The Type C drawings are about 136 years old.
- Under life + 70 the Type C artwork is free if the artist died before 1956. That is likely for someone working in 1890, but it is not proven.

### How to tell the types apart (checked by eye, at thumbnail size)

- Type A and Type B: trump XXI shows a dancing couple beside a house.
- Type C: trump XXI shows a seated man in oriental dress with a woman and a crescent moon.
- The škis (harlequin holding a small harlequin on a plate) looks the same in all three.

The catch, for all of them:
- **No cavalier.** Tarok has four courts per suit. Four knights must be drawn or built from other parts. Editing a jack into a knight is the usual shortcut.
- **Different pips.** Tarok keeps only 7–10 in spades and clubs and 1–4 in hearts and diamonds. The "1" is a plain one-pip card, not a decorated ace. The 5–10 red and 1–6 black cards are unused.
- **Reversed rank order in red suits.** In red suits 1 beats 2, 3 and 4 (Memory, see file 01). A standard deck's indices do not show this. The UI may need its own hint.
- **Index letters.** Standard sets print K, Q, J. A Slovenian player expects the picture, or K / D / C / P style marks. Traditional Industrie und Glück pip cards have no corner index at all.
- **Courts look English or French.** They will not match Industrie und Glück trumps if the two are mixed.
- **LGPL (SVG-cards).** Modified SVG files must stay LGPL and the licence text must ship with them. Public domain and CC0 sets have no such condition.
- These sets give no trumps. The 22 taroks still need a source.

## (e) Legal notes (short, not legal advice)

**Copyright term**
- Slovenia: life of the author plus 70 years (ZASP Art. 59). Anonymous works: 70 years after lawful disclosure (Art. 61). Collective works: 70 years after disclosure (Art. 62). Source: Commons, Copyright rules by territory/Slovenia.
- Commons uses the rule of thumb "author died before 1956, or anonymous work published before 1956" for Slovenia.
- Result for this project: 19th-century Type A/B art is safely out of copyright. Type C art (c. 1890) very probably is. A modern redrawing (Piatnik today, Modiano 1995) may have its own, newer copyright.

**Scans of public-domain 2D works (EU)**
- Directive (EU) 2019/790, Article 14: when the term of protection of a work of visual art has expired, "any material resulting from an act of reproduction of that work is not subject to copyright or related rights", unless that material is itself original. Source: EUR-Lex.
- Meaning: a plain museum scan of a public-domain card gets no new copyright in the EU.
- Slovenia: the DSM Directive took effect on 22.10.2022. The Communia tracker says unoriginal reproductions were already unprotected, so no extra rule was needed for Article 14. I did not read the ZASP text itself.
- Austria: Commons says simple photographs of public-domain visual art are not protected.
- France: Commons calls the case law "inconclusive".
- UK: Commons cites the Court of Appeal in THJ v Sheridan (November 2023). No new copyright in a photographic reproduction of a public-domain artwork. The UK is outside the EU, so Article 14 does not apply there.
- Wikimedia position: "faithful reproductions of two-dimensional public domain works of art are public domain" (WMF statement, 2008, on the PD-Art page).
- Limit: Article 14 removes copyright. It does not cancel a website's terms of use or a museum's contract terms. Taking images from a site that says "all rights reserved" still invites a dispute. Prefer sources that state public domain themselves.

**Public Domain Mark vs CC0**
- CC0 is a waiver by the rights holder. The Public Domain Mark is a label that says "we believe this is free of known copyright". It is not a licence and not a guarantee. Source: CC FAQ.
- The Volkskundemuseum decks carry the mark, not CC0. The practical risk is low, because the museum made the assessment and published it.

**Attribution in a repository (CC BY, CC BY-SA)**
- Give Title, Author, Source, Licence (TASL). Link to the source and to the licence. Say if the file was changed (cropped, resized, recoloured). Source: CC wiki and FAQ.
- Attribution may sit on a credits page. For a repo that means a `CREDITS.md` or an `assets/cards/README.md`, plus a visible "Credits" screen in the app.
- Public-domain and CC0 files need no credit by law. Museums ask for one. Give it anyway.
- Example line: `Tarock XXI, Ferd. Piatnik & Söhne, Wien, 1903–1912. Volkskundemuseum Wien, ÖMV/87529. Public Domain Mark 1.0. Source: https://sammlung.volkskundemuseum.at/sammlungen/content/titleinfo/201122. Resized.`

**Does a CC BY-SA asset force the code licence?**
- No. CC FAQ: a collection that includes a BY-SA work does not have to be under SA. The BY-SA work keeps its own licence.
- A program that loads image files is normally treated as a collection of separate works, not an adaptation of the images. This is the common reading, not a court ruling (Inferred).
- If a BY-SA image is edited, the edited image must stay BY-SA.
- CC advises against CC licences for software. Use a software licence (MIT, Apache-2.0, GPL) for the code.
- Usual layout: `LICENSE` at the root for code. A separate licence note in the assets folder. A credits file that lists each source. The REUSE convention does the same with a `LICENSES/` folder and either `.license` sidecar files or one `REUSE.toml`. Source: REUSE FAQ.
- Precedent: Tarok Palčka. AGPL code. Card sources and licences listed in `tarok/README.md`.

**Trade marks and names**
- Old Piatnik cards carry the maker's name on the ace of hearts and two jacks (museum inscription data). Piatnik is a live brand. A README line such as "not affiliated with Piatnik" is cheap insurance (Inferred, not from a source).

**AI-generated art**
- EU: no specific rule. The test is "the author's own intellectual creation", which needs human creative choices. Purely AI-made output probably has no copyright. Sources: European Parliament study (July 2025) and EPRS briefing (December 2025), both seen only as listings.
- US: the Copyright Office report of January 2025 says the same. Prompts alone do not make the user the author (Listing).
- Result: the student may be unable to licence AI output as "own work". Others could copy it freely. The model may also reproduce protected designs, for example the modern Piatnik look.
- The course requires AI use to be declared (stated in the task brief, course rules not read by me).

Medium if traced from a modern Piatnik card, because tracing copies protected expression. Trace from pre-1900 scans only. | Poor to medium. Scenes vanish at small sizes. Needs a big numeral on top anyway. |
| 3. Hybrid. Own index-only frame, with a cropped public-domain scene (rows 1–3 of table b) as a small centre picture on taroks and courts. | Medium. Cropping and cleaning 38 pictures, plus the frame. | Low. Sources are marked public domain. Must list sources. The scenes are Type A/B, so they differ from the deck players own. | Good. The numeral carries the meaning. The picture is decoration. |
| 4. Public-domain scans used as full card faces (rows 1–2). | Low. Download, resize, name files. | Low for copyright. Product risk: aged paper look, no corner indices, not Type C. Repo size grows if large files are committed. | Poor. Old cards have no index and small pips. Hard to read in a fan on a phone. |
| 5. Adapt an open French-suited set for the suits (section d), with own or public-domain taroks. | Medium. Four cavaliers to draw. Indices to change. | Low with public domain or CC0 sets. LGPL adds conditions. | Good for pips. Courts are busy at small sizes. |
| 6. AI-generated art. | Low to medium. Many retries to keep 54 cards consistent. | Highest. Must be declared for the course. Copyright in the output is unsettled, so "own work" is doubtful. May imitate protected decks. Text and numerals often come out wrong. | Depends on the style. Detailed scenes have the same problem as option 2. |

## (g) Recommendation

**This section is a recommendation, not a finding.**

**Recommended: option 1, index-only vector cards drawn by the student.**
- It is the only route with no third-party rights at all.
- It reads best on a phone, which is where a tarok hand of 12 or 16 cards is hardest to show.
- It can be generated from a small data table, so it is cheap to change later.
- Put the card art under CC0 or CC BY 4.0 and the code under a software licence. State both in the README.

**Optional upgrade: option 3, add public-domain scenes as centre pictures.**
- Source: Volkskundemuseum Wien ÖMV/87529 (Piatnik, 1903–1912, CC Public Domain Mark 1.0, about 2300 × 4100 px).
- Do it as a second "classic" skin after the game works. Keep the index-only skin as default.
- List every file in a credits file. Keep the museum credit even though the mark does not require it.
- Accept that the scenes are not the Type C scenes Slovenian players know.

**Fallback if drawing time runs out:**
- Suits: Byron Knoll's Vector Playing Cards (public domain) for pips and K/Q/J. Cavalier made by editing the jack and adding a "C" mark.
- Taroks: plain numeral cards, or the Noto Sans Symbols2 SVG set from Commons (OFL) which already has knights and trumps 1–21.

**Do not use:**
- Scans or photos of the modern Piatnik deck.
- The Commons "Študentski servis Maribor" set (1995 Modiano deck with a public-domain tag that does not fit).
- WOPC, V&A or dealer images (no open licence).
- British Museum images (CC BY-NC-SA, non-commercial).

**Before committing any third-party file:** open its page again, save the URL and the licence text in the credits file, and note the date.
